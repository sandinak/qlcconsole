## Why

A big moment only feels big if something smaller came before it. Light
can make the audience feel the chorus coming, land a stomp harder, and
tell them when to applaud. Used everywhere at once, it does none of it.

## How light builds momentum

- **Build** into the chorus; land it on the downbeat (the "1").
- **Hold back**: save a colour, the full stage or the movers for the
  final chorus.
- **Hit** the accents: short and sharp, then back.
- **On time:** a cue a beat late feels wrong.
- **Snap or fade:** a snap says "now"; a fade is a breath.
- **Button** the ending: a snap to a big look or to black on the last
  note.
- **Pace the show:** don't peak in song one. Ballads rest; the closer is
  biggest.
- **Restraint:** fewer, better cues.

## Steps

1. Listen to the song again. On your Lesson 0.5 song map, mark:
   - **↗** each build, **★** each hit;
   - **S** or **F** at every change;
   - **PEAK** on the biggest moment;
   - the **button**: to a look, or to black?
2. Check every change has a feel beside it. Hand the map in: it is part 1
   of the Level 0 check.
3. Pace a set list (opener, ballad, up-tempo, feature solo, closer): an
   energy level 1–5 each, and where the show peaks.

## Exit ticket

1. **Snap or fade?** The last note of a big finish; the start of a
   ballad's first verse.
2. Why hold something back in the first chorus?
3. What is a **button**, and what does it tell the audience?

## At home

Do one:

- **Song map:** round 2 of your at-home song map: builds, hits, S / F,
  peak, button.
- **Paper:** a five-song set list, each with an energy level 1–5, and
  where the show peaks and why.
