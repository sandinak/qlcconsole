# Lesson 0.6 — Anticipation and momentum

**Student handout**

## Why this matters

A big moment only feels big if something smaller came before it. Light
can make an audience feel the chorus coming before it arrives, land a
stomp so it hits harder, and end a number so they know to applaud. Used
everywhere at once, it does none of that. Timing and restraint are what
make a show feel like it's going somewhere.

## Today you will

- learn how light builds anticipation and keeps momentum;
- add builds, hits and the button to your song map from Lesson 0.5;
- hand in the song map: part 1 of the Level 0 check.

## Words for today

| Word | Means |
|---|---|
| **Build** | Light that grows over several bars, into a bigger moment: more lights, brighter, more colour, faster. |
| **Holding back** | Keeping something in reserve (a colour, the full rig, the movers) so a later moment can be bigger. |
| **Hit** (or **sting**) | A sudden, short change on a musical accent: a stomp, a drum hit, a held note. |
| **Snap** | A change with no fade: a hit, a surprise, a button. |
| **Fade** | A change over time: a breath, a mood shift. |
| **Button** | The final hit that ends a number: a snap to a big look, or to black, on the last note. |
| **Peak** | The biggest moment of a song, or of the whole show. |
| **Pacing** | How the energy rises and falls across a whole show. |

## How light builds momentum

- **Build into the chorus.** The pre-chorus grows; the chorus arrives on
  the downbeat, not before.
- **Hold back.** Don't use everything in chorus 1. Keep a colour, the
  full stage or the movers for the final chorus.
- **Hit the accents.** A stomp, a key change, a held note: a short,
  sharp change, then back.
- **Time it to the music.** A cue on the beat or the lyric feels right; a
  beat late feels wrong. Changes land on the **downbeat** (the "1"),
  not half-way through a bar.
- **Snap or fade.** A snap is a hit; a fade is a breath. A slow fade can
  carry a whole verse; a snap says "now".
- **Button the ending.** The audience applauds when the light tells them
  the number is over. A snap to a big look, or to black, on the last
  note.
- **Pace the show.** Don't peak in song one. The opener is big, a ballad
  lets the audience breathe, the closer is the biggest. A blackout means
  more after a song that used it once than after one that used it ten
  times.
- **Restraint.** Fewer, better cues. Every cue should have a reason.

## Add to your song map

On your Lesson 0.5 song map, mark:

- **↗** each build (where it starts, where it lands);
- **★** each hit, with the accent it's on;
- **S** or **F** beside every change: snap or fade;
- **PEAK** on the biggest moment;
- the **button**, and whether it snaps to a look or to black.

Then check: does every change have a feel written beside it? Hand it in.
