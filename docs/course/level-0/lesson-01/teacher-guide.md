# Lesson 0.1 — Why we light a show

**Teacher guide** · Level 0, lesson 1 · 25 minutes · no computers ·
pairs with Lesson 0.2 in one period

**Give students:** <https://sandinak.github.io/qlcconsole/class/0-1/> (the lesson's ideas, the activity, the exit ticket and the at-home task; nothing else)

<img src="../../images/qr/class-0-1.svg" width="120" alt="QR code for https://sandinak.github.io/qlcconsole/class/0-1/">

## Why this lesson comes first

Most of the class has never thought about stage lighting as a choice.
Before anyone touches a console they need the reason the console exists:
light does jobs for the performance. Every later lesson, app lessons
included, opens with the job its step does. Assume nothing; define every
word the first time.

## Objectives

Students can:

1. say what a show loses without stage lighting;
2. name the six jobs of stage light (visibility, focus, mood, time and
   place, story, rhythm) and give a show choir example of each;
3. name the four crew roles between a design and the stage (designer,
   programmer, stage manager, board op).

## Vocabulary (introduce in this order)

**work light**, **look**, **cue**, the six jobs (**visibility**, **focus**,
**mood**, **time and place**, **story**, **rhythm**), **lighting
designer**, **programmer**, **stage manager**, **board op**. Each is in
the handout and the [Glossary](../../GLOSSARY.md).

## Timing

| Min | Part | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| 0–5 | **Hook** | Work light vs show light |
| 5–12 | **The six jobs** | The table, a show choir example each |
| 12–20 | **The clip** | Watch once; name the job of each change |
| 20–23 | **The crew** | Designer → programmer → SM → board op |
| 23–25 | **Exit ticket** | Three questions (or fold into 0.2's) |

## 1. Hook (5 min)

Show the **work light** picture. *"This is our stage with every light up
evenly. What can you see?"* (Everything.) *"What is it telling you?"*
(Nothing.) Then the **solo** picture. *"Same stage. Where do you look? How
does it feel?"*

**Key idea to land:** work light shows everything; show light chooses.
Every choice is somebody's decision, and that is the crew's job.

## 2. The six jobs (7 min)

Go down the table on the handout. For each job, ask for a moment from a
show they've seen before giving the handout's example. Push on two:

- **Visibility comes first.** A beautiful look where you can't see the
  faces has failed. In show choir the faces sell the song.
- **Focus works by contrast.** The soloist reads because the rest of the
  stage is dimmer, not only because the soloist is bright (Lesson 0.4).

## 3. The clip (8 min)

Play the clip once. Students log changes in the handout table: *when*,
*what the light did*, *which job*. Pause once if they're behind. Take
three answers; accept any job they can justify. Most changes do two jobs.

## 4. The crew (3 min)

Walk the four steps on the handout. The course trains all four: designing
on paper (Level 0), programming (Levels 1–2), calling and running (1.5,
0.7), and the real rig (Level 3). On our crew, one person may design and
program.

## 5. Exit ticket (2 min)

[Exit ticket](exit-ticket.md), or ask the questions aloud and take it with
0.2's if the two lessons share a period.

## Extension

*"Our show opens with 'Everybody Scream'. Which two jobs matter most in its
first ten seconds, and why?"*

## At home

[At home](at-home.md): notice light doing a job outside the theatre (a
shop window, a game, a concert stream) and log two.

## Answer key

- **Handout, work light vs show light:** 1 — everything, evenly: every
  person, the risers, the trusses. 2 — look at the soloist (focus), that
  this is a solo moment (story), a quiet, exposed feel (mood); faces lit
  (visibility).
- **Exit ticket:** 1 — any three of visibility, focus, mood, time and
  place, story, rhythm. 2 — focus (and story). 3 — the lighting designer
  decides; the board op takes the cue (the SM calls it; the programmer
  builds it).
