# Lesson 0.5 — Lighting a song

**Teacher guide** · Level 0, lesson 5 · 25–30 minutes · no computers ·
pairs with Lesson 0.6 in one period

**Give students:** <https://sandinak.github.io/qlcconsole/class/0-5/> (the lesson's ideas, the activity, the exit ticket and the at-home task; nothing else)

<img src="../../images/qr/class-0-5.svg" width="120" alt="QR code for https://sandinak.github.io/qlcconsole/class/0-5/">

## Why this lesson

Lesson 1.1 starts with "a song has sections; a cue is where the light
changes". This is where students learn to hear those sections and to
decide, before any equipment, what each one should feel like. The song
map they make here is the cue sheet's first draft.

## Objectives

Students can:

1. name a song's sections (intro, verse, pre-chorus, chorus, bridge,
   dance break, final chorus, button) and hear them;
2. say why neighbouring sections should look different (contrast), and
   that every change needs a reason (one look per idea);
3. describe how a designer works before choosing lights: listen, story,
   feel, staging, then lights.

## Before class

- [ ] **The song (teacher picks).** Best: **"Everybody Scream"**, our
  number, as the choir sings it, so the map is the start of the real
  design. Otherwise a show choir number or pop song of 3–4 minutes with
  clear sections. Have a way to play it with sound and to pause.
- [ ] If you can: a rehearsal video of the choreography, or ten minutes
  with the choreographer's notes on formations. Lighting the staging is
  half of this lesson.
- [ ] The projector for the [slides](slides.md) and
  `images/lesson-1-1/rig-verse.png` and `rig-chorus.png`.
- [ ] Print the [handout](handout.md) (students keep it for 0.6) and the
  [exit ticket](exit-ticket.md).

## Vocabulary (introduce in this order)

**intro**, **verse**, **pre-chorus**, **chorus**, **bridge**, **dance
break**, **final chorus**, **button**, **song map**, **contrast**. Each
is in the handout and the [Glossary](../../GLOSSARY.md).

## Timing

| Min | Part | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| 0–5 | **Verse vs chorus** | Two pictures; why they differ |
| 5–10 | **How a designer works** | The five steps; the sections |
| 10–15 | **Listen 1** | Play the song; students mark the sections |
| 15–23 | **Listen 2 + song map** | Play it again; fill in staging and feel |
| 23–25 | **Exit ticket** | Or ask aloud and collect with 0.6 |

## 1. Verse vs chorus (5 min)

Project `rig-verse.png` then `rig-chorus.png`. *"Which is the verse? How
do you know?"* (The verse is a soft warm glow on the step tops; the
chorus fills the risers in saturated magenta: more light, more colour.) **Key idea to land:** the light follows the song's shape.

## 2. How a designer works (5 min)

The five steps on the handout. Stress step 4: show choir is
choreography. The light must be where the singers will be, and a
formation change is often the right moment for a cue: the change in the
picture and the change in the light land together. A designer watches
rehearsal and talks to the choreographer and director before writing a
cue.

## 3. Listen 1 (5 min)

Play the song. Students write only the **section names** and when each
starts (a time or the first words). Pause after the first chorus and
check they have intro, verse, pre-chorus (if there is one) and chorus.

## 4. Listen 2 and the song map (8 min)

Play it again. Students fill in where the singers are (from the
rehearsal video, the choreographer's notes, or "imagine it"), what the
audience should feel, and a one-line look idea. Collect three "feel"
answers for the chorus and the bridge on the board; argue for contrast.

## 5. Exit ticket (2 min)

[Exit ticket](exit-ticket.md) — answers below. Students **keep the song
map** for Lesson 0.6.

## Extension

*"Where in this song would you change the light for the choreography,
not the music?"* (A formation change, a feature stepping out, the dance
break.)

## At home

[At home](at-home.md): a song map of a song of their own choice.

## Answer key

- **Song map:** sections in order with a start for each; a feel in every
  row that differs from its neighbours; look ideas that follow the rules
  of thumb (verse smaller than chorus, bridge new, final chorus biggest).
  For "Everybody Scream", Lesson 1.1's first cues are the model: a pose
  before the first note, the verse, the chorus.
- **Exit ticket:** 1 — any four sections in a sensible order. 2 — so the
  chorus feels bigger (contrast), and the audience feels the song's
  shape. 3 — listen; know what the song is about; decide what each
  section should feel like; watch the staging (any two).
