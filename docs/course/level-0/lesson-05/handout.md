# Lesson 0.5 — Lighting a song

**Student handout**

## Why this matters

A show choir number tells its story in sections. The verse sets up, the
chorus pays off, the bridge turns somewhere new. If the light looks the
same all the way through, the audience loses the shape of the song. If
it changes for no reason, they lose the singers. A designer's first job
is to know the song well enough to say where the light should change,
and why, before choosing a single light.

## Today you will

- learn the sections of a song and what each usually needs;
- learn how a designer works before touching any equipment;
- listen to a song and make a **song map**: where the light changes, and
  what the audience should feel.

## Words for today

| Word | Means |
|---|---|
| **Intro** | The music before the singing. Often a pose, or the choir moving into place. |
| **Verse** | Tells the story. Each verse has new words; the music repeats. |
| **Pre-chorus** | A short lift into the chorus. |
| **Chorus** | The hook: the same words each time, usually the biggest part. |
| **Bridge** | A section that sounds different, near the end, before the last chorus. |
| **Dance break** | Music with no singing, for choreography. |
| **Final chorus** | The last, biggest chorus, often with a key change. |
| **Button** | The final hit that ends a song. |
| **Song map** | A sheet listing a song's sections, when each starts, what the stage is doing and what the audience should feel. |
| **Contrast** | How different two moments are. A quiet verse makes the chorus feel bigger. |

## How a designer works

1. **Listen** to the song, several times, with the lyrics.
2. **What is it about?** One sentence. What changes from start to end?
3. **Feel each section.** What should the audience feel there?
4. **Watch the staging.** Where are the singers in each section? Who has a
   feature? When does the formation change? Talk to the choreographer:
   the light has to be where the singers will be, and a formation change
   is often the best place for a cue.
5. **Then the lights.** Only now: which looks make those feelings?

## Rules of thumb

- **Each section looks different from its neighbours.** Contrast is what
  makes the chorus land.
- **One look per idea.** Change the light when the song changes, not
  because you can.
- **The verse is closer than the chorus**: fewer lights, smaller, faces.
- **The chorus opens up**: more of the stage, more colour.
- **The bridge goes somewhere new**: a colour or direction not used yet.
- **Save something for the final chorus.**
