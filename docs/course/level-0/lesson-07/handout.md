# Lesson 0.7 — Running a show: cues, calls and busking

**Student handout**

## Why this matters

A perfect cue at the wrong moment is a wrong cue. In performance the
board op can't always see or hear what's coming, so the crew runs on a
plan, the **cue list**, and a shared language: the stage manager warns,
then calls, and the op answers. And live shows don't always go to plan:
the band vamps, a soloist holds a note, something goes wrong. Then the
op **busks**: runs the light by feel, on the music, until the show is
back on the list.

## Today you will

- learn how a cue list is run: standby, GO, and who says what;
- learn what busking is, and when and why an operator busks;
- run a song from cue cards on the calls, then busk a vamp.

## Words for today

| Word | Means |
|---|---|
| **Cue list** | All of a song's cues, numbered, in order. Run the same way every performance. |
| **Call** | The stage manager's spoken instruction that makes a cue happen. |
| **Standby** | The warning before a call. The op answers "Standing by" and gets ready. |
| **GO** | The word that makes the cue happen, said on the exact moment. |
| **Busking** | Running lighting live by feel, choosing looks and hits on the music instead of following a fixed cue list. |
| **Vamp** | A section the band repeats until the singers are ready to go on. It can last any length. |
| **Hit** | A look kept ready to fire by hand: for busking. |

## Who does what

| Role | Before the show | During the show |
|---|---|---|
| **Lighting designer** | Designs every look and cue; writes the cue sheet | Watches; gives notes after |
| **Programmer** | Builds the cues into the console | Usually not needed |
| **Stage manager (SM)** | Writes the calls into the script or score | Watches the stage and **calls** every cue |
| **Board op** | Learns the show | Takes each cue on the call; busks when needed |

## The call

| Who | Says |
|---|---|
| SM | "Standby lights cue 5." |
| Op | "Standing by." |
| SM | "Lights cue 5 … GO." |
| Op | Takes the cue on **GO**, not before. |

The standby comes early enough (about 10–15 seconds) for the op to be
ready. The GO comes on the exact moment: the downbeat, the lyric, the
stomp.

## Cue list or busking?

| Cue list | Busking |
|---|---|
| Planned, rehearsed, the same every night | Live, by feel, different every night |
| For anything the song does the same way each time | For anything it doesn't: a vamp, a soloist who goes long, an encore, a dance-off, a problem on stage |
| The SM calls it | The op chooses, on the music |

Most shows use both: the cue list runs the show, and a few hits are kept
ready on top of it.

**Busking well:**

- know the song: count the bars, hear the accents coming;
- keep a small set of hits ready, planned in advance;
- less is more: a hit on every beat is noise;
- go back to the cue list as soon as the show is back on it.

## Run it

**Round 1, the calls.** The SM holds the song map from Lesson 0.5–0.6
and calls each change. The op flips a cue card on each GO. Switch roles
after one run.

**Round 2, busking.** The teacher loops one section (a vamp). The op has
three hit cards (for example: *Red, full stage*; *Blackout*; *Strobe on
the risers*) and plays them on the accents. Then the SM calls "Go back
to the list" and the op returns to the next cue.
