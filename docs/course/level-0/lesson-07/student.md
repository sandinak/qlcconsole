## Why

A perfect cue at the wrong moment is a wrong cue. The crew runs on a
plan, the **cue list**, and a shared language. When the show doesn't go
to plan, the op **busks** until it's back on the list.

## Who does what

| Role | During the show |
|---|---|
| **Designer** | Watches; gives notes after |
| **Programmer** | Built the cues beforehand |
| **Stage manager (SM)** | Watches the stage and **calls** every cue |
| **Board op** | Takes each cue on the call; busks when needed |

## The call

| Who | Says |
|---|---|
| SM | "Standby lights cue 5." |
| Op | "Standing by." |
| SM | "Lights cue 5 … GO." |

Go on **GO**, never on the standby.

## Cue list or busking?

The **cue list** is for everything the song does the same way each time.
**Busking** is for what it doesn't: a vamp, a soloist who goes long, an
encore, something going wrong. Most shows use both.

Busking well: know the song, keep a few hits ready, less is more, go
back to the list as soon as you can.

## Steps

1. **The calls:** the SM calls each change on the song map; the op holds
   up the cue card on GO. Switch roles.
2. **Busking:** the section loops. The op plays three hit cards on the
   accents, then the SM calls the next cue and the op goes back to the
   list.

## Exit ticket

1. The SM says "Standby lights cue 5." What do you say, and what do you
   do next?
2. What is **busking**?
3. Name **two** moments in a show choir performance when the operator
   might busk.

## At home

Do one:

- **Call a song:** someone at home flips a switch on your GO. Three
  changes, standby first. Swap.
- **Paper:** on your song map, number the cues and mark each standby and
  the exact moment of each GO. Mark one place you'd keep a hit ready, and
  why.
