# Lesson 0.7 — Running a show: cues, calls and busking

**Teacher guide** · Level 0, lesson 7 · 25 minutes · no computers ·
the rest of the period: finish the Level 0 check part 1, or start
Lesson 0.8

**Give students:** <https://sandinak.github.io/qlcconsole/class/0-7/> (the lesson's ideas, the activity, the exit ticket and the at-home task; nothing else)

<img src="../../images/qr/class-0-7.svg" width="120" alt="QR code for https://sandinak.github.io/qlcconsole/class/0-7/">

## Why this lesson

Most students have never heard the word "busking", and few know a stage
manager calls the lighting. This lesson gives them the whole picture of
a show in performance before they learn the tools: Lesson 1.5 runs the
Run tab on the calls, and Lesson 2.6 builds the hits they busk with.

## Objectives

Students can:

1. say who does what on a lighting crew before and during the show;
2. give and take a standby and a GO correctly;
3. say what busking is, and name two moments when an operator busks.

## Before class

- [ ] The song from Lessons 0.5–0.6, and a way to loop one section (a
  vamp): a music player's loop, or just restart it.
- [ ] **Cue cards:** one card per change on a song map, numbered (index
  cards are fine), one set per pair. And three **hit cards** per pair,
  for example *Red, full stage*, *Blackout*, *Strobe on the risers*.
- [ ] Optional, **a clip (teacher picks)**: a stage manager calling a
  show (several theatres and schools post "calling the show" videos), or
  an operator busking a concert. Check it before class. Look for: the
  standby coming well before the GO, and the GO landing on the moment.
- [ ] Print the [handout](handout.md) and the [exit ticket](exit-ticket.md).

## Vocabulary (introduce in this order)

**cue list**, **call**, **standby**, **GO**, the four roles (**designer**,
**programmer**, **stage manager**, **board op**), **busking**, **vamp**,
**hit**. Each is in the handout and the [Glossary](../../GLOSSARY.md).

## 1. Who does what (5 min)

The table. On our crew one person may be designer and programmer, and
the SM may be a teacher at first. The board op never decides when a cue
goes in a called show: the SM does, because the SM sees the whole stage.

## 2. The call (4 min)

Practise it as a class: you are the SM, they are the ops. "Standby lights
cue 5." (*"Standing by."*) Wait. "Lights cue 5 … GO." Do one where you
pause a long time before GO: anyone who moves early has jumped the cue.
**Key idea to land:** the op goes on the word GO, never on the standby.

## 3. Round 1: the calls (7 min)

Pairs: SM with a song map, op with the cue cards. Play the song; the SM
calls each change, the op holds up the card on GO. Switch once.

## 4. Busking (7 min)

Explain with the handout's table. Show choir moments: the band vamps
while the choir changes formation; a soloist holds a note far longer
than in rehearsal; a competition encore; a mic fails and the director
stretches a section. Busking is not "making it up": the hits are
planned, the op knows the song, and returns to the list as soon as they
can.

Round 2: loop a section. Ops play their three hit cards on the accents,
then you call "Back to the list: standby lights cue 6 … GO."

## 5. Exit ticket (2 min)

[Exit ticket](exit-ticket.md) — answers below.

## Extension

*"The soloist goes long and the band vamps. You are the op. What do you
do with the light, and when do you go back to the cue list?"*

## At home

[At home](at-home.md): call a song to someone at home, or write the calls
for a song map.

## Answer key

- **Exit ticket:** 1 — "Standing by"; then wait for GO. 2 — running
  lighting live by feel, firing looks and hits on the music instead of a
  fixed cue list. 3 — any two: a vamp, a soloist who goes long, an encore,
  a dance-off, something going wrong on stage.
- **Extension:** keep the soloist lit; busk small, planned hits on the
  vamp's accents (or hold the look); go back to the list on the SM's
  call, or at the next planned cue.
