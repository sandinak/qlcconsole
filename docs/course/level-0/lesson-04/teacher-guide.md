# Lesson 0.4 — What the audience reads

**Teacher guide** · Level 0, lesson 4 · 25 minutes · no computers ·
pairs with Lesson 0.3 in one period

**Give students:** <https://sandinak.github.io/qlcconsole/class/0-4/> (the lesson's ideas, the activity, the exit ticket and the at-home task; nothing else)

<img src="../../images/qr/class-0-4.svg" width="120" alt="QR code for https://sandinak.github.io/qlcconsole/class/0-4/">

## Why this lesson

Students design from the inside ("I want pink"). The audience reads from
the outside ("I'm looking at her; this feels lonely"). This lesson turns
them round. It is the reason behind Lesson 1.2 (colour and intensity)
and 1.3's solo.

## Objectives

Students can:

1. name what pulls the eye: brightness, contrast, movement, strong
   colour, faces;
2. say how to pick out a soloist (bring them up, bring the rest down);
3. say what a colour or a direction usually tells an audience, and that
   the song decides.

## Vocabulary (introduce in this order)

**contrast**, **ratio**, **silhouette**, **blackout**, **saturated /
pale**. Each is in the handout and the [Glossary](../../GLOSSARY.md).

## Timing

| Min | Part | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| 0–5 | **Where's your eye?** | Three pictures, first reactions |
| 5–12 | **What pulls the eye** | The list; the soloist ratio |
| 12–20 | **What colour and direction say** | The table; the swatch sort |
| 20–22 | **Felt, not seen** | A cue the audience feels |
| 22–25 | **Exit ticket** | On paper |

## 1. Where's your eye? (5 min)

Project each picture for five seconds. *"Point: where did you look
first? One word: how does it feel?"* Write the words on the board.

## 2. What pulls the eye (7 min)

The list on the handout. Then the soloist: *"How do you make one singer
stand out of 24?"* Most say "a brighter light on them". Land the other
half: **and take the rest down**. The solo picture works because the cyc
and the risers are dark.

## 3. What colour and direction say (8 min)

The table. Swatch sort (if you have cards): groups sort the swatches
into "warm / cool" and pick one for a lullaby, a breakup verse, a
celebration, and "Everybody Scream". Then push back on the table: *"Is
blue always sad?"* (No: a bright saturated blue on a dance break reads
as cool in the other sense.) **The song decides.**

## 4. Felt, not seen (2 min)

*"Did you notice the light fading down in the clip's bridge?"* Usually
not, and they still felt it go quieter. Some cues are meant to be seen
(a snap, a blackout); most are meant to be felt.

## 5. Exit ticket (3 min)

[Exit ticket](exit-ticket.md) — answers below.

## Extension

On the back of the handout: the opening pose of "Everybody Scream" as a
silhouette. Which parts of the look (Lesson 0.3) are on, and which are
off?

## At home

[At home](at-home.md): find a shot where the light picks out one
performer and say how, or sort colours for songs on paper.

## Answer key

- **Read three pictures:** verse — the blue cyc, with the singers as dim
  shapes under a faint lavender uplight; cold, eerie, distant (and no
  faces: Lesson 1.3 adds the front light). Scream — the red over everything; danger,
  violence, energy. Solo — the lit singer downstage; alone, exposed,
  important. Accept any answer with a reason.
- **Exit ticket:** 1 — any three of brightest place, contrast, movement,
  strong colour, faces. 2 — bring the soloist up **and** the rest down.
  3 — mystery, shape, anonymity (a silhouette); accept "dramatic" with a
  reason.
- **Extension:** background (the cyc) lit; back light if any; front light
  and specials **off**, so the singers are dark shapes.
