## Why

The audience never sees your cue sheet. They notice where they're
looking and how a moment feels. Every choice you make is a message, so
you need to know how they read it.

## What pulls the eye

Strongest first:

1. the **brightest** place;
2. the most **contrast**: a lit face on a dark stage;
3. **movement**;
4. a **strong colour** in a pale stage;
5. **faces**, if they're lit.

To pick out a soloist, bring them **up** and the rest **down**. The ratio
matters more than the level.

## What colour and direction say

| | Usually says |
|---|---|
| Warm (amber, warm white) | Close, friendly, day |
| Cool (blue, lavender) | Calm, cold, night, alone |
| Saturated red / magenta | Energy, danger, passion |
| Pale, near white | Open, exposed |
| Front light | "Here I am" |
| Back light, silhouette | Mystery, shape |
| Side light | Drama, the body |
| Blackout | An end, a shock, a reset |

Habits, not rules: the song decides.

**A cue is a change the audience feels.** A snap on a stomp is meant to
be seen; a slow fade into the bridge is meant to be felt.

## Exit ticket

1. Name **three** things that pull the audience's eye.
2. A soloist doesn't stand out, though their light is at Full. What do
   you change?
3. The number opens with the choir in **silhouette**. What does that tell
   the audience?

## At home

Do one:

- **Watch:** find a moment where the light picks out one performer. How
  did it do it, and what did it make you feel?
- **Paper:** a colour, and why, for a lullaby, a breakup verse, a
  celebration finale and a villain's entrance.
