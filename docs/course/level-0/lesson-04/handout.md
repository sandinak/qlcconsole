# Lesson 0.4 — What the audience reads

**Student handout**

## Why this matters

The audience never sees your cue sheet. They don't know what a cue is.
They only notice where they're looking and how a moment feels. Every
choice you make is a message to them, so you need to know how they read
it: what pulls the eye, what a colour says, what a blackout means.

## Today you will

- learn what pulls the audience's eye;
- learn what colour and direction say;
- read three pictures as an audience member, then as a designer.

## Words for today

| Word | Means |
|---|---|
| **Contrast** | The difference between two things: bright against dark, warm against cool, one moment against the next. |
| **Ratio** | How much brighter one place is than the rest. A soloist reads at a high ratio. |
| **Silhouette** | A performer seen as a dark shape against a lit background. |
| **Blackout** | Every stage light out at once. |
| **Saturated / pale** | Deep, strong colour / colour with white mixed in. |

## What pulls the eye

In order of strength, the eye goes to:

1. **the brightest place** on stage;
2. **the most contrast**: a lit face against a dark stage;
3. **movement**: a beam sweeping, a light that changes;
4. **strong colour**: one saturated colour in a pale stage;
5. **faces**, always, if they are lit.

To pick out a soloist, bring them **up** and bring the rest **down**. The
ratio matters more than the level.

## What colour and direction say

| | What it usually says | In a show choir number |
|---|---|---|
| Warm (amber, warm white) | Close, friendly, day, safe | A love song, a hometown song |
| Cool (blue, lavender) | Calm, cold, night, alone | A lullaby, a breakup verse |
| Saturated red / magenta | Energy, danger, passion | "Everybody Scream" |
| Pale, near white | Honest, open, exposed | A quiet a cappella moment |
| Front light | "Here I am": open, direct | Faces sell the lyric |
| Back light, silhouette | Mystery, shape, anyone | The opening pose |
| Side light | Drama, the body | The dance break |
| Blackout | An end, a shock, a reset | The last note; a sudden silence |

These are habits, not rules. The song decides. A cold colour in a love
song says something too, if you mean it.

## A cue is a change the audience feels

Some changes should be **noticed** (a snap on a stomp). Most should be
**felt**: a slow fade that makes the bridge quieter without anyone seeing
it happen. If the audience watches the lights instead of the singers when
they shouldn't, the cue is too loud.
