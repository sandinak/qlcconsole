# At home — Lesson 2.2

Do **one** of these.

## A. On your computer (Mac, Windows, or Linux)

1. Download [`lesson-2-2-finish.qlcc`](../../shows/lesson-2-2-finish.qlcc)
   (on GitHub, open it and click **Download raw file**) and open it.
2. Give the verse a colour of the song's own, the same way: right-click
   **Lavender** → **Duplicate**, name it `Verse Colour` in the Look
   Editor, and move it to the **01-Everybody Scream** folder (right-click
   → **Move to folder** → **01-Everybody Scream**).
3. Click **1.0.1 Verse** and drag **Verse Colour** onto its **StepTops**
   row.
4. Open it with **✎** and pick a verse colour you like better. Check
   **Used by** first.
5. Save it as `Lesson2-2-Home-yourname.qlcc` and bring it in, or hand in
   a screenshot of the Library with your song folder open.

## B. On paper (no computer, or a Chromebook)

Plan the palettes for a song you know (or "Everybody Scream").

1. List four to six palettes **by job**, not by colour: *Verse Colour*,
   *Chorus Colour*, *Cyc Colour*, *Solo Level*… For each, write its value
   now (a colour or a level) and the cues that will use it.
2. The director gives a note: "the verse is too cold". Write which
   palette you change, and every cue that changes with it.
3. In a sentence: why is a palette per song safer than changing the
   show's own Lavender or Blue?
