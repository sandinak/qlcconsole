# Glossary

Every term the course uses, with the lesson that introduces it in
brackets.

## The stage

| Word | Means |
|---|---|
| **Upstage / downstage (US / DS)** | Toward the back / the front of the stage. (Old stages sloped, so the back was "up".) [0.8] |
| **Centre stage (CS)** | The middle of the stage; on our risers, the middle row. [0.8] |
| **Stage left / stage right (SL / SR)** | The singers' left / right as they face the audience. Stage right is the audience's left. [0.8] |
| **Risers / steps** | The platforms the choir stands on: three rows (US, CS, DS) of four units, numbered 1–4 from SR. Their lights are the **Steps** (strips on the fronts) and **StepTops** groups. [0.8] |
| **Cyc** (cyclorama, pronounced "sike") | The large white cloth across the back of the stage, lit to fill the background with colour. [0.8] |
| **Truss** | A metal frame that lights are rigged on. Our **Trusses** are four upright ones across the back: two SR, two SL (1 = outside). [0.8] |
| **AT (audience tower)** | A tower of lights in the house, one each side (AT1 SR, AT2 SL), lighting the singers from the front. [0.8] |
| **UST** | Upstage beam: the eight moving beams along the top riser. [0.8] |
| **RGB / W** | A pixel's red-green-blue colour LEDs / its white LEDs. *TrussC-RGB* is the colour pixels of the boxes lighting the **C**yc; *TrussW-RGB* those **W**ashing the stage. [1.1] |
| **House** | Where the audience sits. **Front of house (FOH)**: lights out there, pointing at the stage. [0.8] |
| **Rig** | All the lighting fixtures and everything they hang on. [0.8] |
| **Proscenium** | The frame around the stage opening, where the main drape hangs. [0.8] |
| **Practical** | A light that is part of the set and is seen as a light by the audience: a hanging lantern, a lamp on a table, a light-up staff. Our **Lanterns** are practicals. [3.3] |
| **House lights** | The lights over the audience. Up before the show and at intermission, out when the show starts. [3.7] |
| **Work lights** | Plain bright lights for working on stage when there's no show: rigging, rehearsal set-up, cleaning. [3.7] |
| **Stage marks** | The lines a crew spikes and focuses by: the centre line, the curtain line, the number line and the apron. In the Lighting Studio they are on the plot's **Stage** layer (Plot layers), each with its own eye and lock. [3.6] |
| **Curtain line** | Where the main curtain hangs, across the stage: the line up- and downstage distances are measured from (the plaster line). [3.6] |
| **Number line** | A line across the front of the stage, numbered in feet out from the centre line (**CL**), so the crew can say how far left or right something is: "SL 4". [3.6] |
| **Apron** | The part of the stage in front of the curtain line. [3.6] |
| **Stage manager (SM)** | Runs the show during performance and **calls** every cue. [0.1, 1.5] |
| **Board operator (board op)** | Runs the lighting console, taking each cue when the SM calls it. [0.1, 1.5] |
| **Lighting designer (LD)** | Decides what every look and cue is, and why. [0.1] |
| **Programmer** | Builds the designer's looks and cues into the lighting console. [0.1] |

## Light

| Word | Means |
|---|---|
| **Intensity** | How bright a light is, from Full (100 %) to Out (0 %). [0.2] |
| **Colour** | The colour of the light: warm (amber, warm white) or cool (blue, lavender), saturated or pale. [0.2] |
| **Direction** | Where light comes from: front (shows faces), back (outlines), side (sculpts), below (drama). [0.2] |
| **Movement** | Light that changes while you watch: fades, chases, moving beams. [0.2] |
| **Visibility** | The first job of light: the audience can see the performers, especially their faces. [0.1] |
| **Focus (of the audience)** | Where the audience looks. The eye goes to the brightest part of the stage. [0.1] |
| **Mood** | The feeling of a moment. [0.1] |
| **Time and place** | A job of light: saying when or where we are. Deep blue for night, a sunrise wash for a new day. [0.1] |
| **Story** | A job of light: showing that something has changed. The choir freezes in silhouette when the lyric turns. [0.1] |
| **Rhythm** | A job of light: moving with the music. A snap on a stomp; the chorus bigger than the verse. [0.1] |
| **Work light** | Plain, even light for working on stage: everything visible, nothing chosen. (On the rig, see *Work lights*.) [0.1] |
| **Energy** | How big the music is at a given moment; the lighting follows it. [0.1] |
| **Warm / cool** | Warm colours (amber, warm white, red) read as friendly or hot; cool ones (blue, lavender, cyan) as calm, cold, night-time or eerie. [1.2] |
| **Saturated / pale** | Deep and strong (Red, Blue) / softer, with white mixed in (Lavender). [1.2] |
| **Contrast** | The difference between two things: bright against dark, warm against cool, one moment against the next. A dark verse makes the chorus that follows feel bigger. [0.4, 1.2] |
| **Ratio** | How much brighter one place is than the rest. A soloist reads when they are up **and** the choir is down. [0.4] |
| **Front light (key) / fill** | The main light on faces, from in front and a little above / softer light from the other side so the key's shadows aren't black. Our audience towers fill each other. [0.3] |
| **Front / back / side light** | Light from the audience side (shows faces) / from behind (outlines performers against the background) / from the side (sculpts bodies; the dance break's light). [0.3, 1.3] |
| **Snap** | A change with no fade (0 seconds), for a hit, a surprise or a button. [0.2, 0.6, 1.4] |
| **Wash** | A soft, wide light that covers an area evenly: the whole choir in warm amber. [0.3, 1.1] |
| **Special** | A light for one person or one place: the soloist downstage centre. [0.3] |
| **Background** | What is behind the performers; ours is the cyc, which carries most of the stage's colour. [0.3] |
| **Spot** | A narrow, hard-edged light, often following one person. [1.3] |
| **Shutter** | A spot's built-in shutter blocks the beam: while it's closed, no light comes out, however high the level. Our AT-Spots' shutter opens by itself when they're lit; Shutter palettes still close it or strobe it. [1.3] |
| **Position / pan / tilt** | Where a moving light points: it pans (turns left and right) and tilts (up and down). [1.3] |
| **Mover / moving light** | A fixture that can be pointed (panned and tilted) from the console. [1.3] |
| **Pixel box / pixel bar** | A fixture made of many small LEDs (pixels), each of which can be a different colour. [1.1] |
| **Uplight** | Light from below: dramatic or eerie. Our riser tops uplight the singers. [0.3, 1.1] |
| **Silhouette** | A performer seen as a dark shape against a lit background: mystery, shape. A choir in silhouette for the opening pose. [0.4, 1.1] |
| **Eye candy** | Light that's there to be looked at (beams, pixels) rather than to light the performers. [2.4] |

## Songs and shows

| Word | Means |
|---|---|
| **Song map** | A sheet of a song's sections: when each starts, where the singers are, what the audience should feel, and (0.6) its builds, hits and button. The first draft of a cue sheet. [0.5] |
| **Intro / verse / pre-chorus / chorus / bridge / dance break / final chorus** | A song's sections: the music before the singing / tells the story / the lift into the chorus / the hook, usually biggest / the different section before the end / music for choreography / the last and biggest chorus. [0.5] |
| **Build** | Light that grows over several bars into a bigger moment: the pre-chorus lifting into the chorus. [0.6] |
| **Holding back** | Keeping something in reserve (a colour, the full stage, the movers) so a later moment can be bigger: the final chorus. [0.6] |
| **Sting** | A short, sharp change on a musical accent: a stomp, a drum hit, a key change. Also called a hit. [0.6] |
| **Downbeat** | The first beat of a bar, the "1". Cues land on it. [0.6] |
| **Peak / pacing** | The biggest moment of a song or show / how its energy rises and falls. Don't peak in song one. [0.6] |
| **Button** | The final hit that ends a song: a snap to a big look, or to black, on the last note. It tells the audience to applaud. [0.5, 0.6, 1.1] |
| **Blackout** (in a song) | Every stage light out at once: an end, a shock, a reset. Means more for being rare. (The console's switch: see *Hardware*.) [0.4, 0.6] |
| **Call** | The SM's spoken instruction that makes a cue happen: "Lights cue 5 … GO." [0.7, 1.5] |
| **Busking** | Running lighting live by feel, firing looks and hits on the music instead of a fixed cue list: for a vamp, a soloist who goes long, an encore, a problem on stage. Most shows keep a few hits ready over the cue list. [0.7, 2.6] |
| **Vamp** | A section the band repeats until the singers are ready; it can last any length. [0.7] |

## The Show Tree

| Word | Means |
|---|---|
| **Production** | The whole show: every cue list in it. With one production, GO plays it; with more than one, the **current** production is the one GO plays. [1.1] |
| **Cue list** | One song's cues, numbered and in order, run the same way every performance. [0.7, 1.1] |
| **Cue** | A planned lighting change, tied to a moment in the song: a change the audience feels. [0.1] Numbered 1.0.0, 1.0.1…; a list plays its cues in number order. [1.1] |
| **Point cue** | A cue numbered between two others, such as 1.0.2.5 between 1.0.2 and 1.0.3. It is how a cue is added late without changing any other cue's number. [1.5] |
| **Renumber** | Give cues new numbers. Dragging a cue renumbers it for its new place; **Renumber this level…** numbers a whole level 1, 2, 3… Do it before anyone has written the numbers down. [1.5] |
| **Group** | A named set of lights that are always used together, like *Steps* or *TrussC-RGB*. [1.1] |
| **Layer** | One part of a look doing one job [0.3]; in the Show Tree, one group (or several) doing one thing in a cue. [1.1] |
| **Look** | Everything the lights are doing at one moment [0.1]; in the Show Tree, what a layer does, one value per attribute: colour, intensity, position… [1.1] |
| **Palette** | A saved, named value — a colour, a level, a position — that you drag onto a layer. Change the palette and every cue using it changes. [1.1] Name it for its job (*Chorus Colour*), so it stays true after a note. [2.2] |
| **Used by** | Right-click a palette in the Library: every cue and layer that uses it (and any VC button that fires it). Check it before changing a palette. [2.2] |
| **Look Editor** | Opens under the Show Tree's page (**✎** on a layer's row, or **Edit** in the Library) to change a palette's value and name; it says *Used by* and that a shared palette changes everywhere. [2.2] |
| **Group set** | One name for several groups, used as one layer: *Truss Sides* = Trusses SR + Trusses SL (Library: select the groups, right-click → **Make set…**; All → Group sets). A set's layer is one layer: it can't be split, but its parts can have child layers. [2.3] |
| **Child layer** | A layer on part of its parent (one member of its set, or a group inside it), shown **↳** under it: it sets only what it sets, and the parent lights the rest. ⇧ as you drop, or right-click the layer → **Add child layer**. [2.3] |
| **Split / Merge** | Split: a layer on several groups becomes a layer per group, same looks. Merge (**More ▾ → Merge**): several layers of one cue become one. [2.3] |
| **Symmetry** | Both sides of the stage lit the same: balanced. Breaking it draws the eye. [2.3] |
| **Palette folder** | A folder in the Library's Palettes; a folder per song (*01-Everybody Scream*) keeps the song's own palettes together. **Duplicate** copies a palette into one. [2.2] |
| **⋯ (more)** | The button at the end of each row of a layer's page: **Set to**, **Level…**, **Colour…** and more. [1.2] |
| **One-off value** | A level or colour you picked (Level…, Colour…) that isn't a palette: kept just for one layer. **Make reusable** turns it into a palette. [1.2] |
| **Cue look** | A palette dropped on the cue itself (its dashed box): it reaches every layer of the cue that doesn't set that attribute itself. [1.2] |
| **Undo / Redo** | ⌘Z reverses the last change on the tab in front; ⇧⌘Z reapplies it. On a tab with nothing to undo, **Edit → Undo** reads *Nothing to undo on this tab*. [1.2] |
| **Library** | The panel on the left: groups, targets and palettes to drag. [1.1] |
| **Rig Overview** | qlcconsole's 3D view of the stage, showing what the lights are doing. It's our stage in class. [0.8] |
| **Tracking** | A light holds its state into the following cues until a cue changes it. A **tracked** value shows in grey: *tracked from 1.0.0*. [1.1] |
| **S / T / R / M / F** | The Show Tree's columns, each named when you hover its header. On a cue, **S** (Sets) counts the layers it sets, **T** (Tracks in) the layers tracking in, **R** (Releases) the layers it lets go; on a layer, a dot marks which. **M** (Mode): **B** Block, **C** Cue-only. **F** (Flags): base, skip, no MIB and the like. [1.1] |
| **GO** | Plays the next cue. In the **Run** tab: the green button or Space. [1.1] |
| **Run tab** | The operator's screen: the cue list, NEXT, and big GO / Back / Stop buttons. [1.1] |
| **Design / Operate** | Design: you build, and the selected cue shows on the stage. Operate (the Run tab says **LIVE**): you run the show; only GO changes the stage. The button is at the top right. [1.5] |
| **Standby / LOAD** | The warning before a call [0.7]. In the Run tab, clicking a cue LOADs it as **▷ STANDBY**, the cue the next GO plays. [1.5] |
| **Back / Stop** | Back: the cue before. Stop: stops the list; the stage goes dark. [1.5] |
| **Stage target** | A named place on stage (Soloist DS, Center) that moving lights can aim at. Library → All → Targets. [1.3] |
| **Aim** | Point moving lights at a stage target: drop the target on the layer. The Position row then says *Aim Center*. [1.3] |
| **Block cue** | A cue that starts from a dark stage, with nothing tracking into it (Mode: Block; **B** in the tree's **M** column). [1.3] |
| **Fade** | How long a change takes. **Fade in**: how long a cue takes to arrive; **fade out**: how long its lights take to go when the next cue takes over. [1.4] |
| **Hold / follow** | How long a cue waits before the next one plays by itself (∞: waits for GO). A cue that plays by itself **follows**. [1.4] |
| **Tracked in / Next cue receives** | On a cue's page: each layer tracking into the cue, where from and its values / what the cue after it starts from. [2.1] |
| **Release** | Ticked on a layer: it leaves when its cue is left, back to what the base cue plays on it, else dark. The cue's **R** column counts them. [2.1] |
| **Cue-only cue** | A cue that starts from the one before but whose changes don't pass on (Mode: Cue-only; **C** in the **M** column): a flash or sting that's there and gone. [2.1] |
| **Hard start** | A cue that starts from a known state whatever came before: a Block cue, for a solo or a new section. [2.1] |
| **Base cue** | The cue a list's released layers go back to (tick *The list's base cue* on its page; **base** in the **F** column). [2.1] |
| **Effect** | A pattern that moves through the lights: a chase, a sparkle, a rainbow. [2.4] |
| **Hit** | A short, sharp change on a musical accent [0.6]; a look kept ready to fire by hand over the running list, for busking [0.7]: the Show Tree's **Hits** section, built with **+ Hit**. A hit never tracks. [2.6] |

## Hardware (Level 3)

| Word | Means |
|---|---|
| **DMX** | The signal that tells real lights what to do: 512 channels per **universe**. [3.1] |
| **Address** | Where a light's channels start in its universe. [3.1] |
| **Patch** | Which address each light is at. [3.1] |
| **Art-Net / sACN** | DMX sent over a network cable. [3.1] |
| **Fixture definition** | The file that tells the app what each channel of a light does. [3.2] |
| **Plot symbol** | What a fixture looks like on the plot and in the studio: a moving head, a PAR can, an LED bar, a lantern… Set per fixture; **Auto** draws it from its type. [3.3] |
| **Relay** | A switch the console controls, for power: it is either on or off, never dimmed. Used for a circuit that powers LED fixtures, or for house lights on a relay panel. [3.7] |
| **Grand Master (GM)** | The one fader over everything: at 50 % every light is at half its level. [3.7] |
| **Blackout** | A switch that takes every light to zero at once, whatever is running; press it again to bring them back. [3.7] |
| **Blind** | Building without the real lights showing it: the rig is muted while the screen preview stays live. Design mode only. [3.7] |
| **Output rules** | Per-fixture rules for circuits that aren't show lights: ignore Blackout, ignore the Grand Master, switch like a relay, on when not in use. [3.7] |
| **Focus** (the rig) | Pointing, sizing and sharpening each light so it lands where the design says, once per room, before the cues run. [3.6] |
| **Focus call** | A step of the focus: some lights and the point they focus to ("Truss-Spots → Center"). The show keeps its calls in order; run them from the controller. [3.6] |
| **Hot spot** | The brightest part of a beam, usually its centre. "Hot spot on me": the centre of the beam on the designer's face. [3.6] |
| **Home** | Where a moving light points with no position given: pan and tilt at the middle of their travel. [3.6] |
| **Spot down / flood out** | Make a beam narrower / wider (its zoom). [3.6] |
| **Sharp / soft** | A crisp / blurred edge on a beam (the focus of its lens). [3.6] |
| **Correction** | How far a light is off in one room (pan and tilt, in degrees), kept so the cues still land on their marks. [3.6] |
| **Venue** | One room the show plays in. Its corrections, truss heights, target positions, house rig and outputs are kept in a file beside the show (`Show.venues.xml`), never in the show. The footer's venue chip says which is applied (**Venue: Classroom**). [3.6] |
| **House rig / tour package** | The lights a room already has / the lights that travel with the show. [3.6] |
| **Owner** | Whose a light is: **Tour** (travels with the show; the default), **Rental** (hired, travels too) or **House** (the room's own, saved with that venue). [3.6] |
| **Focus report** | The record of a focus: every light, its point, its correction, who checked it and when, and any dead lamp. [3.6] |
| **Scramble** | A practice: every moving light is knocked off its target by a random amount, and you focus them back, scored on degrees off, time and saves. Your real venues are never touched. [3.6] |
| **Focus this cue** | Changing a cue's own values (position, zoom, focus, colour, gobo) from the stage with the controller (on a Build scene: Focus this look). Venue focus fixes the rig for a room; Focus this cue changes the cue for every room. [3.6] |
| **Quick focus** | Setting the rig's own calibration, a head at a time, into the show. [3.6] |
| **Venue focus** | Running the show's focus calls in a new room, the corrections kept for that venue. [3.6] |
| **Special circuit** | On an Eos desk, a channel that isn't a stage light: a relay, house lights, work lights, backstage blues. [3.10] |
| **Sub (submaster)** | On an Eos desk, a fader that brings up a set of levels by hand. An imported sub becomes a Virtual Console fader. [3.10] |
