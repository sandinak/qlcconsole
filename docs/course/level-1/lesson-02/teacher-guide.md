# Lesson 1.2 — Colour and intensity tell the mood

**Teacher guide** · Level 1, lesson 2 · 50 minutes

**Give students:** <https://sandinak.github.io/qlcconsole/class/1-2/> (the show file, the steps, the exit ticket and the at-home task; nothing else)

<img src="../../images/qr/class-1-2.svg" width="120" alt="QR code for https://sandinak.github.io/qlcconsole/class/1-2/">

**Builds on:** Lessons [0.4](../../level-0/lesson-04/teacher-guide.md), [0.6](../../level-0/lesson-06/teacher-guide.md). Open with the handout's *Why this matters* before any clicks.

## Objectives

Students can:

1. say how colour (warm / cool, deep / pale) and intensity change the
   mood of a moment, and why contrast between sections matters;
2. change a look in place with **⋯ → Set to** and **⋯ → Level…**, and
   keep a new level with **Make reusable**;
3. give a whole cue one colour and level with a **cue look**, and take
   back a step with **Undo**.

## 1. Design idea (10 min)

1. **Mood sort.** Groups get the swatch cards (or the list on the
   handout) and sort them under four moods: *calm*, *happy*, *eerie*,
   *angry / exciting*. There's no single right answer: ask *why*.
2. Draw out the two scales:
   - **Warm ↔ cool**: amber and warm white feel friendly and close; blue
     and lavender feel cold, night-time, distant.
   - **Saturated ↔ pale**: deep red shouts; pale pink whispers.
3. **Intensity** is mood too: *bright isn't always better*. A dim stage
   feels secret or sad; the chorus feels bigger if the verse was darker.
   That's **contrast**.
4. **The director's note** (read it out): *"The verse is too cosy for
   'Everybody Scream'. Make it feel cold and a little eerie. And when
   they scream, I want the whole stage to go red."* Students fill in the
   handout's cue sheet: what changes in the verse, and the new cue 1.0.3
   *Scream*.

**Key idea to land:** every change of colour or level is a change of
mood. Change it on purpose, and make the sections different from each
other.

## 2. Teacher demo (10 min)

On the projector, the class telling you what to click:

1. Click **1.0.1 Verse**, then its **StepTops** row on the page: the
   layer's own page, one row per attribute.
2. On the **Colour** row, **⋯ → Set to → Lavender**. Read the note in the top bar
   aloud: *"Changed: 1.0.1 Verse, 1.0.2 Chorus"*. "Why did the chorus
   change? (*StepTops tracks into it.*)"
3. On the **Dimmer** row, **⋯ → Level…**, type **40**, Apply. The row says
   *40% · 1.0.1 Verse StepTops* and **Make reusable**: "40% isn't one of
   our palettes, so it's a **one-off**, just for this layer."
4. **Undo** (⌘Z) and **Redo** (⇧⌘Z): "Nothing is permanent. Try things."

Stop there; the students do the rest, including the scream.

## 3. Hands-on (20 min)

- Groups of 2–3 with **Driver / Navigator / Designer** roles; switch after
  each part of the handout.
- Students without a laptop: the **design station** — the mood sort with
  the swatch cards, then plan a colour for each of the five parts of the
  song (intro, verse, chorus, scream, ending), then swap.

### What to watch for

| You see | Likely cause | Say |
|---|---|---|
| No ⋯ buttons | The cue's page is showing, not the layer's | "Click the **StepTops** row on the page first." |
| The palette went on the wrong layer | Dropped on a row instead of the dashed area (or the other way) | "Undo, and read the note in the top bar while you drag: it says where it goes." |
| The Scream's rows have no colour after the drop | Red was dropped on the grey row of another layer | "Drop it on the **dashed area**, below the rows." |
| "My chorus changed too!" | Tracking | "Good! StepTops carries on until a cue changes it." |
| A strip with a slider at the bottom | **Level…** | "Type 40 and click Apply." |

## 4. Check for understanding (while circulating)

- "Which feels colder, the old verse or the new one? Why?"
- "Why does the row say *1.0.1 Verse StepTops* after 40%?" (*It's a
  one-off value, just for this layer, until you make it reusable.*)
- "What's the difference between dropping Red on a row and on the dashed
  area?" (*A row: that layer only. The dashed area: a cue look, every
  layer of the cue that doesn't have its own colour.*)

## 5. Share (5 min)

Two groups click through Pose → Verse → Chorus → Scream on the
projector. The class says what each one feels like. Is there **contrast**
between the verse and the chorus?

## Answer key

- **Exit ticket:** 1 — cool (blue/lavender), dimmer: calm, cold, eerie;
  any reasoned answer. 2 — the layer's ⋯ on the Colour row → **Set to**
  (or drag a palette onto the row). 3 — it's a value that isn't one of our
  palettes, kept just for that layer (a one-off); **Make reusable** makes
  it a palette. 4 — a **cue look**: a colour or level for the whole cue,
  reaching every layer of the cue that doesn't set its own.
- **Extension puzzle:** the hint says *"reaches 0 of 1 layers"*: the
  Pose's only layer, TrussC-RGB, has its own colour (Blue), and a layer's
  own value beats a cue look.
